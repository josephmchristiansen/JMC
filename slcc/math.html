Magnets & Pain Project

Applying statistical analysis to real-world healthcare research

Project Overview

Brief Description

This project analyzed the effects of static magnetic fields on chronic pain in post-polio patients. Using statistical methods such as mean, range, histograms, and box plots, we compared pain ratings before and after treatment with either an active magnet or a placebo device. The study aimed to determine whether magnets have a measurable impact on pain relief.

Reflection

This project investigated whether magnets can really help with pain relief by analyzing Dr. Vallbona's study data. I examined the pain levels before and after using either a real magnet or a fake one (placebo). To understand the results, I created histograms, box plots, and calculated averages and ranges. These tools helped me determine if there was an actual difference between the two groups. It was a practical application of statistical math to evaluate a real medical claim.

Do you think this project shows how math can be applied to the real world?

Yeah, for sure! I realized while working on this project that math actually matters outside of class. With all the claims about whether magnets work or not, these statistical tools gave us a way to look at the actual evidence. In healthcare especially, you need solid proof before recommending treatments, and math provides that proof. It was cool to see how the statistical methods from our textbook could help evaluate a real medical treatment.

Can you give an example of another application where this type of analysis would be beneficial?

This same kind of math would be super helpful in testing new medicines. Like, if researchers want to know if a new pain reliever actually works better than a sugar pill, they'd need to look at the averages, differences, and patterns in their data — pretty much exactly what was done in this magnet study. Without statistical analysis, they'd have no way to tell if the medicine was actually doing anything. I guess this is why drug companies have to run so many trials before getting FDA approval.

If you were trying to decide if magnets work, why would it be important to explain the details of this project to clients?

If I was telling clients whether magnets work or not, I'd definitely want to walk them through how the study was designed and what the data showed. People trust facts more than opinions, and showing them the whole analysis builds credibility. Breaking down the research methods and showing the actual data is way more convincing than just saying "trust me, they work" or "they don't work." Plus, when clients understand how we got our results, they're more likely to believe what we found.

Did this assignment change your opinion of the usefulness of math?

I wouldn't say it completely changed my opinion, but it definitely reinforced what I already thought about math being useful. Seeing statistics applied to something concrete like pain management made it click even more for me. It's not just problems on a page; it's a way to find real answers to questions that affect people's health. Before this, I understood math was important in theory, but now I can actually point to an example where statistical analysis helps evaluate a treatment that might actually help people feel better.
